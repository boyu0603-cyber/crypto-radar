# Evaluation Rubric

This rubric evaluates Crypto Radar review items as market intelligence artifacts, not trading signals.

| Criterion | Description | Strong Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source traceability | The item preserves where the information came from. | Source type, date, provenance status, and review notes are included. |
| Relevance clarity | The item explains why the information may matter. | A clear relevance hypothesis is stated without turning it into advice. |
| Uncertainty disclosure | Missing or unclear information is made explicit. | Unknowns, source limits, and alternative interpretations are listed. |
| Timeliness | The item can be reviewed while the information is still useful. | Date, follow-up state, and review cadence are visible. |
| Narrative context | The item connects the event to broader public market narratives. | Related themes are identified without hype or overclaiming. |
| Actionability for review | The item supports a human follow-up decision. | Review questions and possible next states are included. |
| Human review readiness | The item is structured for human judgment. | The output separates summary, hypothesis, uncertainty, and decision state. |
| Public-safety compliance | The item avoids private or sensitive information. | No signals, accounts, credentials, private sources, or proprietary logic appear. |

## Scoring Approach

Each criterion can be scored from 1 to 5:

- 1: Weak or missing
- 2: Present but inconsistent
- 3: Functional baseline
- 4: Strong and repeatable
- 5: Mature and adaptable

## Portfolio Interpretation

For this public repository, the rubric demonstrates how radar items could be reviewed responsibly. It does not claim that Crypto Radar is deployed, profitable, or suitable for automated trading.
